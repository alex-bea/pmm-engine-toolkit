# Marketing Brief Launch Tiers

Use the tier to calibrate marketing effort. The tier does not authorize research, spending,
publishing, or deliverables not approved for the launch.

## Tier 1 — Market-expanding or business-critical launch

### Definition

A launch that materially changes the addressable market, category position, revenue motion,
core product, or strategic partnership footprint. It usually has high complexity, a longer
timeline, broad audience impact, and significant organizational investment.

### Typical goals

- Drive meaningful adoption, pipeline, or conversion.
- Establish or change market perception.
- Build credibility for a major product or partnership move.

### Expected preparation

- New or refreshed audience and market analysis.
- Approved messaging and positioning.
- Strong proof and qualified review for material claims.
- Cross-functional launch and channel planning.

### Typical channel breadth

Multiple owned, earned, partner, community, sales, event, and paid channels as approved.

### Use when

- Business impact or reputational stakes are high.
- The launch creates a new product, category, audience, or revenue motion.
- Research and cross-functional coordination are substantial.
- The launch requires the broadest campaign investment.

## Tier 2 — Meaningful feature, segment, or awareness launch

### Definition

A launch that adds important customer value, expands a segment, or creates a meaningful
education and awareness opportunity without redefining the business. Complexity, timeline,
and investment are moderate.

### Typical goals

- Increase awareness and understanding.
- Drive adoption of a meaningful feature or use case.
- Strengthen positioning with an existing audience.

### Expected preparation

- Existing audience context, refreshed when needed.
- Approved launch messaging and product facts.
- Supporting proof appropriate to the claims.
- Coordination across several relevant channels.

### Typical channel breadth

Usually three to five approved channels across owned content, social, lifecycle, community,
partner, sales, or paid media.

### Use when

- The feature or segment matters but does not redefine the market.
- Complexity and timeline are moderate.
- Several teams or channels need a shared brief.
- The classification is genuinely ambiguous and no approved tier is supplied.

## Tier 3 — Small update or visibility moment

### Definition

A focused, lower-impact release, integration, community moment, or maintenance update that
can be communicated with low effort and little or no incremental budget.

### Typical goals

- Keep current users informed.
- Maintain useful visibility or credibility.
- Support adoption with minimal coordination.

### Expected preparation

- Existing approved messaging and audience context.
- Confirmed product facts.
- A compact brief with no new strategy unless separately requested.

### Typical channel breadth

Usually one or two approved owned, lifecycle, social, community, partner, or sales channels.

### Use when

- Scope and complexity are low.
- Turnaround is short.
- The launch serves an existing audience with a small change.
- The communication can reuse approved strategy and messaging.

## Classification order

1. Use an explicitly approved tier when supplied.
2. Otherwise assess business impact, audience change, complexity, timeline, research needs,
   channel breadth, and investment.
3. If the result remains ambiguous, use Tier 2.
4. Record the selected tier in Brief Info. Do not add a tier justification section to the
   finished brief.
