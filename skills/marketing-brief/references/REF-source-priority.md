# Marketing Brief Source Priority

## Core rule

Use the highest-priority source that directly owns the field. Do not merge conflicting
claims, invent missing facts, or let a lower-priority note override an approved decision.
Use `[Missing]` when a required fact is absent or remains unresolved.

## Default source order

1. Final PRD or approved product specification
2. Approved launch plan or campaign brief
3. Approved product, PMM, or go-to-market strategy
4. Approved messaging or positioning
5. Launch-tier framework
6. Launch-specific customer, partner, or sales notes
7. Meeting notes, brainstorms, or chat summaries

## What each source owns

### 1. Final PRD or approved product specification

Use for product behavior, launch scope, feature details, availability, requirements, and
constraints. Do not treat implementation prose as approved marketing messaging or proof.

### 2. Approved launch plan or campaign brief

Use for the launch summary, launch type, owner, timing, named audiences, major channels, and
success measures.

### 3. Approved product, PMM, or go-to-market strategy

Use for market context, audience framing, problem statement, and strategic rationale.

### 4. Approved messaging or positioning

Use for the topline message, support points, approved proof, CTA language, and terminology.
Product facts still come from the product specification.

### 5. Launch-tier framework

Use for tier classification, effort, likely channel breadth, expected research depth, and
typical deliverable categories. Do not use it for launch-specific facts or claims.

### 6. Launch-specific customer, partner, or sales notes

Use for observed pain, customer language, use cases, and objections. Treat the material as
directional unless an approved source confirms a public claim.

### 7. Meeting notes, brainstorms, or chat summaries

Use for open questions, early ideas, and working assumptions. Do not treat them as final truth
when a higher-priority source owns the field.

## Field ownership

| Field | Default owner |
|---|---|
| Product behavior and scope | Final PRD or approved specification |
| Launch timing and channels | Approved launch plan |
| Market problem and audience rationale | Approved strategy |
| Messaging, CTA, and approved terminology | Approved messaging or positioning |
| Launch tier | Explicit approved tier, then tier framework |
| Customer example or language | Approved customer record or approved brief |
| Metric, proof point, or claim | The approved source that explicitly states it |

## Conflict rules

- Resolve each field independently; one document does not win every field.
- Product specification wins product-fact conflicts.
- Approved launch plan wins timing and channel conflicts.
- Approved messaging wins wording conflicts but cannot change product facts.
- An explicitly approved tier wins; otherwise apply the tier framework.
- Use a customer example only when its source permits the intended use.
- Use a metric or claim only when an approved source states it clearly.
- If equal-authority sources conflict, use `[Missing]` and request a decision.

## Missing-information rules

- If launch type or tier cannot be determined, use the documented Tier 2 default.
- If an audience is implied but not named, use `[Missing]`.
- If a required proof point is unsupported, use `[Missing]`.
- If an optional proof or support point is unsupported, omit it.
- If the CTA is not explicit, use `[Missing]`.
- Never use the fictional example to fill a real user's missing field.

## Tier-sensitive evidence

- **Tier 1:** Expect strong approved evidence and formal source material. Do not rely on
  assumptions for business-critical claims.
- **Tier 2:** Prefer approved launch materials, existing audience context, and approved
  messaging. Preserve meaningful gaps.
- **Tier 3:** Work from existing approved material. Keep the brief compact and do not create
  new strategy.
