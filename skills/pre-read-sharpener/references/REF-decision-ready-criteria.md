# Decision-Ready Pre-Read Criteria

## Purpose

This binary rubric defines whether a tightened executive pre-read is decision-ready. Score
the rewrite, not the source draft. Every criterion must pass before normal return.
Partial credit does not exist.

## Four anchors

| Anchor | Required answer | Required location |
|---|---|---|
| Clear audience | Named decision-making role or roles, not a generic group | Audience field |
| Clear decision | One sentence naming the single choice the meeting must make | Decision needed field and The decision |
| Clear tradeoff | What approval gives up and what rejection gives up | Tradeoffs table |
| Clear outcome | The concrete supported change if the meeting approves the recommendation | What changes if we say yes |

A missing or vague anchor fails its related criteria. Use `[Missing]` rather than inventing
support.

## Pass/fail rubric

| # | Criterion | Pass condition | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single thesis upfront | The decision-forcing question appears within the first 100 words. | The reader must search for the decision. |
| 2 | Specific over abstract | Each material claim has a supplied number, named example, dated event, or concrete proof. | Abstract claims appear without source support. |
| 3 | Scannable in two minutes | Rewrite is at most 600 words; paragraphs are short; key text is sparse; decision fields and tradeoffs are prominent. | Dense prose or hidden tradeoffs slow the reader. |
| 4 | Strong opinion | One option is recommended; alternatives are rejected with a supported reason. | Options appear without one recommendation or use hedging. |
| 5 | Plain English and active voice | Direct subject-verb-object sentences dominate; nominalizations and academic language do not. | Passive or academic phrasing obscures action. |
| 6 | No consultant language | The rewrite contains none of: “at the end of the day,” “synergize,” “best-in-class,” “north star,” “table stakes,” “circle back,” or “double-click.” | Any prohibited phrase appears. |
| 7 | Decision and tradeoffs visible | The first 100 words and tradeoff table reveal the choice and what each path gives up. | The tradeoff becomes clear only after deep reading. |
| 8 | No throat-clearing | The rewrite opens with the decision; history appears only when it changes the choice. | Recap or preamble delays the decision. |
| 9 | Cost to reverse stated | The table contains Cost to reverse for the recommended option, using source facts. | The column is absent or omits supplied reversal information. |
| 10 | One deciding question | A decision-call rewrite or agenda names one question matching the Decision needed field. | Multiple, implied, or inconsistent deciding questions appear. |

## Interpretation rules

- Specificity never authorizes invented evidence. A missing supported detail remains
  `[Missing]` and may keep a criterion failing.
- Informational pre-reads require user confirmation before sharpening and do not require a
  decision-call agenda. Their rewrite still uses the template unless the user explicitly
  authorizes a template change.
- A self-check explanation cites visible properties of the rewrite. It does not claim hidden
  reasoning or unsupported confidence.
- A still-failing result after three revision rounds requires the user's explicit choice
  before return.
