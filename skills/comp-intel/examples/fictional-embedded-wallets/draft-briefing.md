## Top signal

**AsterPort is expanding from wallet onboarding toward a bundled wallet-and-money-movement
platform.** It shipped transfer policies and changed its headline from an onboarding message
to an integrated-platform message (Observed: E-001, E-002, E-003). This raises the importance
of HarborKey's policy-continuity position, but HarborKey still lacks approved migration proof
(Inference: C-002; Unknown: C-004). The next action is to validate HarborKey's migration method
before making a switching claim (Recommendation: C-003).

**EmberPass now publicly occupies automated-commerce territory that HarborKey discusses only
in held future positioning.** Its fictional rebrand, product release, and official launch post
all point to programmable spending controls (Observed: E-010, E-011, E-018). HarborKey should
compare currently shipped policy scope and keep the future orchestration claim out of the
response (Recommendation: C-007).

## Competitor activity

### AsterPort

- **What changed — Observed:** AsterPort released per-session transfer limits and approval
  rules and now says “Wallets and money movement in one platform” (E-001, E-002).
- **Why it matters — Inference:** The combined product and narrative changes indicate expansion
  from onboarding toward an integrated commerce platform (C-002).
- **Affected audience/use case:** Application teams evaluating embedded wallets inside an
  existing fictional payments relationship.
- **Positioning coverage:** WEAK. HarborKey has reviewed policy and export claims, but no
  approved migration-time proof or entitlement comparison.
- **Recommended response:** Validate migration proof and compare policy lifecycle rather than
  bundle breadth (C-003).
- **Limitations:** Account export, migration behavior, customer adoption, and transfer-control
  plan entitlement are unknown (C-004).

### BrindleAuth

- **What changed — Observed:** BrindleAuth now leads with institutional controls after its
  fictional acquisition by Ironclad Systems (E-005, E-006).
- **Why it matters — Inference:** Owner credibility may strengthen enterprise evaluations even
  when the wallet product's own proof is incomplete (C-010).
- **Affected audience/use case:** Fintech and procurement-led platform evaluations.
- **Positioning coverage:** MISSING for enterprise scale and support proof; STRONG for HarborKey's
  reviewed export and unified-SDK claims.
- **Recommended response:** Gather approved enterprise-control and support evidence before
  drafting a counter.
- **Limitations:** The 249-unit public plan and 2,400-unit attributed quote may cover different
  packages; no direct price disadvantage is established (E-007, E-015).

### EmberPass

- **What changed — Observed:** The provider rebranded and launched delegated sessions,
  recipient rules, and spending limits for automated commerce (E-010, E-011, E-018).
- **Why it matters — Inference:** The new position overlaps HarborKey's future orchestration
  direction, but only EmberPass has current public shipping evidence in this example (C-006).
- **Affected audience/use case:** Fintech builders and software-agent platforms.
- **Positioning coverage:** CONFLICTING. HarborKey has reviewed current policy claims, while its
  broader orchestration claim remains on hold.
- **Recommended response:** Compare current policy scope and auditability; do not invoke held
  future work (C-007).
- **Limitations:** Customer adoption, production scale, and general availability are unknown
  (C-008).
